Eigenvectors & Eigenvalues

Eigen

Eigenvectors don't change direction under transformation, only scale. This is the key insight that makes diagonalization possible.

Determinant as Area

Geometry

The determinant measures how much a transformation scales area (2D) or volume (3D). Negative determinant means orientation is flipped.

# Determinant = area scaling A = [[2, 1], [0, 1]] det_A = np.linalg.det(A) # = 2 # Unit square scales to parallelogram with area 2

Dot Product Geometry

Vectors

The dot product measures how much two vectors point in the same direction. It's the projection of one onto the other, times the length.

# Dot product as projection a, b = np.array([3, 1]), np.array([2, 2]) dot = np.dot(a, b) # = 8 proj_len = dot / np.linalg.norm(a)

Change of Basis

Basis

Vectors don't change, but their coordinates depend on the chosen basis. Understanding this is key to diagonalization and SVD.

# Change of basis: P^-1 * A * P P = [[2, 1], [1, 1]] # new basis v_standard = [70, -30] v_new = np.linalg.solve(P, v_standard)
